What is Lore

Lore is a personal AI executive assistant that lives in a folder on your computer. Open that folder in the Claude desktop app (Cowork) and the agent inside becomes yours: it learns your role, your team, your stakeholders, and your priorities, then puts that knowledge to work every day.

There is no server and no account to manage. The folder is the brain. Every page of it is a plain file you can open, read, and edit.

What it does for you

The Quest Log

A live action items tracker in your Cowork sidebar. New items Lore finds wait in a triage pile at the top for your one-click decision: do it now, schedule it, delegate it, or delete it. Nothing joins your list without you.

The Herald

Your daily brief: one designed page that sweeps your email, Slack, and Teams, ranks what actually needs you, and holds a drafted reply for each item. Lore never sends anything. You do.

Meeting memory

Drop in a transcript and Lore files the notes, updates its pages on the people involved, logs real decisions, and proposes the action items that were yours to keep.

Prep on demand

Ask for 1:1 prep, meeting prep, or a weekly review and Lore builds it from everything it has learned so far, not from a blank page.

The one rule worth knowing on day 1

Lore drafts and holds; it never sends. Every message, reply, and update waits for your review. Treat it like a very fast chief of staff with no send button.

Getting started

Set up in 4 steps

You need the Claude desktop app and about 15 quiet minutes.

1

Install the Claude desktop app

Download it from claude.ai/download and sign in with your work account. Cowork is the part of the app that lets Claude work inside a folder on your computer. That folder is about to be Lore.

2

Get the Lore folder onto your machine

From your org's shared Lore release folder (ask in the rollout channel for the link), download the latest package zip and the installer for your computer. Double-click the installer and it puts everything in the right place. Mac: right-click the installer and choose Open the first time; Windows may show a SmartScreen prompt, choose "Run anyway".

Prefer doing it yourself? Unzip the package to ~/src/lore on Mac or C:\Users\<you>\src\lore on Windows. Either way: no git, no GitHub, no accounts, and updates arrive through the same folder with Lore applying them for you (see "Getting updates later" below).

3

Point Cowork at the folder

In Cowork, choose the lore folder as your workspace. Everything Lore reads and writes stays inside it.

4

Say hello

Lore notices a fresh install and runs a short onboarding interview: your role, your team, your key stakeholders, the tools you use, how you like to be spoken to, and when you want your daily brief (morning, afternoon, or both). Skip anything that doesn't apply. Pause whenever you want and say "run onboarding" to pick it back up.

What you'll have when the interview ends

  • A master profile of you and your work that Lore reads at the start of every session.
  • A page for each teammate and stakeholder, ready to grow as meetings happen.
  • The quest log pinned in your sidebar, empty and waiting for its first entry.
  • Your daily brief on a weekday schedule, if your channels are connected, and your first Herald delivered on the spot so you see it working.
  • An assistant that already writes in your preferred tone.
Getting updates later

New versions of Lore ship regularly. Say "update Lore" anytime: the agent pulls the latest playbooks and refreshes The Quest Log, and your personal files (your profile, your team pages, your notes) are never touched. No re-downloading, no re-setup.

The Quest Log, up close

New items arrive as proposals. When Lore pulls action items out of a transcript, they land in a triage pile at the top of the tracker, one card at a time. Each card takes one click: Do now, Schedule, Delegate, or Delete. That click is the decision; there is no second step.

Add your own at the top. The add form sits above the list: subject, who it came from, when it's due, and optional notes. Due dates are ASAP, TBD, a real date, or your own words.

The snapshot ritual. Lore can't see inside the tracker on its own. When you want it to know your current list (for prep, for dedup, for "what should I work on"), click Download snapshot in the tracker and drop the file into the folder's inbox/. One click, done.

Leveling up

Connect your tools

All optional. Lore is useful on day 1; connectors are what make it formidable.

Slack + Microsoft 365

Connect both and say "triage everything." Lore sweeps your inbox, your Slack DMs and mentions, and your Teams chats, then stages a drafted reply for everything that needs one, written in your voice. Slack drafts land in your own Drafts folder; email and Teams drafts are staged as paste-ready files. Every run also delivers the Herald, your designed daily brief (next page). You can put triage on a schedule, for example twice a day.

Plaud recorder

If you carry a Plaud voice recorder, connect its integration and say "sync Plaud from the last week." Lore fetches the recordings it hasn't processed and walks you through them one at a time.

Jira and Confluence

Connect them and Lore grounds its briefs and updates in live tickets instead of guesses, and can write tickets when you ask.

Metabase

If your team's numbers live in Metabase, connect the Metabase MCP and Lore can run your saved questions and pull live results into briefs, preps, and answers. Ask whoever runs your Metabase for the connector details.

Tips from experienced keepers

Be specific

"The quoting integration is blocked on the vendor" beats "team issue." Names, dates, and stakes give Lore something to work with.

Feed the brain

Drop relevant docs into inbox/documents/ and ask Lore to ingest them. The more it knows, the sharper everything downstream gets.

Challenge back

Ask "what am I missing?" or "argue the other side." Lore is at its best when you use it as a sparring partner, not a stenographer.

Edit anything

Every file is plain markdown. If something Lore wrote is wrong, fix the file directly; it reads your edits next session and treats corrections as lessons.

Local-first by design

Every file lives on your machine. Nothing is uploaded unless you ask Lore to use a connector, and updates can never touch your personal files.

For the curious

The advanced shelf

None of this is required. Come back when you're ready for it.

Obsidian mode

If you use Obsidian, Lore can keep its knowledge (people, meetings, decisions, projects) in your vault as linked notes. Each fact lives in exactly one place, backlinks show every meeting a person appears in, and the graph view turns your working life into a map you can explore. Setup takes 3 steps and lives in the README under Obsidian integration. Once it's running, say "lint the vault" for the weekly health check: Lore sweeps for broken links, stale notes, contradictions, and drift, then delivers a styled report of proposed fixes. It never rewrites your knowledge without your sign-off.

Specialist sidekicks

Lore plays well with sibling agents. Every action item carries two flags: Lore (Lore could do this itself) and Specialist (an executor agent could pick it up autonomously). Flag an item Specialist and a companion agent, our reference one is called Sigil, can do the work off-session and mark the item complete in your tracker. The wiring prompt is in the README under Companion specialist agents.

Make it yours

Lore's personality and rules are plain files you're allowed to edit: VOICE.md is the style contract every artifact is checked against, workflows/ holds the playbooks, and CLAUDE.md is the constitution. Change a file and the next session behaves accordingly. A quick map of the folder:

lore/ ├── CLAUDE.md how Lore behaves (read first, every session) ├── VOICE.md the style contract ├── context.md your profile (created by onboarding, private) ├── workflows/ one playbook per task ├── inbox/ drop things here for Lore to process ├── outbox/ briefs, preps, and drafts land here └── team/ stakeholders/ meetings/ decisions/ the memory
