# Starter Talent Framework

> Opt-in. Lore never applies this on its own. Say "use the starter framework" to adopt it, and Lore records it as your talent framework (`workflows/talent-review.md` → step 1). If your company has its own framework, use that instead.

A plain, company-neutral way to look at a direct report's work. It organizes evidence; it does not rate people. There is no scale, no grades, and no categories, because those are where company standards differ most. If your company rates people, use its scale for that part, outside this framework.

## The four parts

| Part | The question | Evidence that counts |
|------|--------------|----------------------|
| **Results** | Did they deliver what was agreed, at the quality agreed? | Outcomes against goals or commitments set earlier; work shipped, stalled, or redone; promises kept or missed |
| **How they work** | How do they get it done with other people? | Collaboration and communication; ownership when things go wrong; reliability; how they handle disagreement and feedback |
| **Capability and growth** | What can they do now, and how fast are they adding to it? | Skills shown in real work; what they learned in the period; how they handled something new; feedback they acted on |
| **Scope and impact** | How far beyond their own tasks does their work reach? | Problems they took on unasked; others they made better; decisions or systems that changed because of them |

Each part gets dated, sourced evidence: strengths and concerns both. Where evidence is thin, say "thin" rather than guessing.

## Two distinctions to keep

- **Over the period vs right now.** A review looks at the sustained record across the whole period. A current read looks at what the person needs this month. Label which one a section is; they can differ.
- **Can't yet vs isn't choosing to.** When results fall short, say which the evidence supports: a skill, clarity, or resourcing gap (fix with coaching, clearer expectations, or support), or a motivation or fit problem (needs a direct conversation). Say what evidence would tell them apart if it's unclear.

## Next moves

For each person, 2 or 3 concrete moves for the next month, tied to the evidence:
- **Results:** reset or clarify expectations, remove a blocker, agree on one measurable goal.
- **How they work:** give specific feedback on one behavior (see `playbooks/giving-feedback.md`), pair them with someone who models it.
- **Capability and growth:** a stretch assignment, a skill to build, a person to learn from.
- **Scope and impact:** a wider problem to own, a chance to teach others, visibility for work already done.

## Reviews

- Feedback happens continuously, close to the moment. Nothing in a formal review should be new to the person.
- A formal review summarizes the period across the four parts, then sets 1 to 3 development goals for the next one.

## What it leaves to your company

Ratings and calibration scales, compensation, promotion criteria, and any HR or legal process for performance problems. When a review touches one of these, Lore says the starter framework doesn't cover it and points you to your company's process.
